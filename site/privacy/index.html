CODELEX privacy policy (Phase 1 Android playtest)

Version playtest-privacy-v1.3 · 16 October 2026

In short

  • CODELEX is a word game about technology. This policy covers the Phase 1 playtest of the Android app (com.codelex.app) and the sign-up form for that playtest. It is not the policy of a public release.
  • The playtest is for adults (18+) who were invited through the sign-up form. The app is shared through a private install link only.
  • Today, nothing leaves your phone. The app has no account, no server, no ads, no analytics service and no crash reporting service. Your matches are saved on your phone so you can resume them.
  • Some features are already built into the app but switched off in the current playtest build (marked “when enabled” below): recording playtest stats on your phone, a one-question survey, and two files you can send yourself, by email, with a tap: your playtest data and a match log for bug reports. Nothing is ever sent automatically.
  • Where your data goes. Everything we receive is kept in Malick BA’s personal Google account (Gmail, Google Forms, Google Drive). Google may store it in several countries, including the United States. We do not store your data in the EU or in any single country we chose.

1. Who is responsible

Malick BA, an individual, is the controller of the personal data described here. Contact for any privacy question or request: titanfst00@gmail.com.

2. Who can take part

You must be 18 or older to take part. You confirm it when you sign up. The app is not meant for children. It is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you are under 18, please do not install the app. If we learn that someone under 18 took part, we remove them from the playtest and delete the data we hold about them. If you think this happened, write to titanfst00@gmail.com.

The app has no age check of its own, because it is only shared with the invited roster. A public version will have an age check before it is released.

3. What we collect, feature by feature

3.1 Signing up for the playtest (now)

Through the sign-up form (a Google Form), we collect: your name, your email address (which we use to send you the install link), a yes/no confirmation that you are 18 or older (not your birth date), your country, your preferred language (English or French), your phone type (Android standard or entry-level), whether you work in software, whether you want a debrief call, and the version and date of the consent you gave.

This list is kept apart from the game data. We never write the app’s tester ID into it.

3.2 Getting the app (now)

The app is not on Google Play. It is an Android app file (APK) built and hosted by Expo Application Services (EAS, run by 650 Industries, “Expo”). We send you a private install link. When you open it, Expo’s servers see the usual technical data of a download, such as your IP address and your device and browser information. Expo handles that data under its own privacy policy. We do not receive install or stability statistics from Google Play or from Expo.

3.3 Playing and resuming a match (now; stays on your phone)

To let you pick up a match where you left it, the app saves on your phone:

  • the match log: the moves played, the random seed of the match, the game and word-list versions, and check values used to detect a damaged file. It contains no time stamp, no identifier and nothing about you;
  • a snapshot of the match, to resume faster;
  • a small resume-safety counter, so that a damaged save shows a “start a new match” card instead of crashing.

Only the match in progress and the last finished match are kept. Older ones are deleted automatically. Your animation choice (Settings, “Animations”) is also saved on your phone.

3.4 “New to you” (now; stays on your phone)

After a match, the summary shows how many terms you played for the first time on this phone. For this, the app keeps on your phone the list of terms you have played (word ids only; no time, no identifier, nothing about the opponent).

3.5 Language (now; stays on your phone, except as in 3.6)

The app reads your phone’s language and region setting (for example fr-SN for French, Senegal) to show English or French. It does not store it on its own. When “Record playtest stats” is on (section 3.6), every recorded event carries this language and region code, so it is in the playtest data file if you send it.

3.6 Playtest data (when enabled)

In Settings, the “Playtest data” section has a switch “Record playtest stats”. It is on by default, because you agreed to it when you signed up; you can switch it off at any time. The first time you open the app, a short notice tells you this. While the switch is on, the app records game events on your phone.

Every event carries:

  • your tester ID (“Your tester ID” in Settings): a random number made by the app on your phone. It is not your phone’s ID. A new one is made if you reinstall the app, or if you switch recording off and on again;
  • the event’s name and format version, a random event id, the time on your phone’s clock, a session id (a new random one each time the app starts), the app version, the platform (Android) and your phone’s language and region code (section 3.5).

The events, in plain words:

  • Opening and leaving the app: each time the app comes to the front, the type of connection (Wi-Fi, mobile, other, none or unknown); after a fresh start, how long the start took and whether the previous session ended unexpectedly while the app was open (a crash, a forced stop or a power loss). When the app goes to the background, how long it was in front.
  • First-launch steps: which first steps you reached (first launch, home screen seen, “PLAY NOW” tapped, first move, first match finished) and the time between them.
  • Matches: a random match id (new for each match); when a match starts: which bot you play against, who plays first, the rules, word-list and bot versions, and whether it is your first match; when it ends: the result, both scores and the difference, how it ended (out of tiles, too many turns without points, or resign), tiles left in the bag and on the board, passes at the end, playing time, number of turns, words, rejected attempts, exchanges and passes, and how many times you resumed it; when a match is dropped: why (you started a new one, a new app version, or a damaged save), turns and playing time; when a match is resumed: from the background or after a restart, how long you were away, how long the restore took and how it was done.
  • Words played by you and by the bot: the term’s id, its form (for example plural), category, difficulty, points, bonuses, your thinking time and the turn number.
  • Rejected attempts: only the reason (not in the word list, bad placement, bad cross-word), the word length, your thinking time and the turn number, never the letters you tried.
  • The bot’s turns: its thinking time and search effort in coarse ranges, the kind of move (word, exchange or pass), and whether it used a fallback or recomputed after a resume.
  • After a match: which button you tapped on the summary (play again, home, discover) and how long you stayed on it.
  • Knowledge Cards and graph: which card you opened and from where, how long you read it and how many related terms you tapped; in the graph, which term you moved from and to, and the link between them.
  • Survey (section 3.7): that the “Would you play CODELEX again?” question was shown, your answer (yes, maybe, no) or that you closed it, and how many matches you had finished.
  • Sending: when you send a file, how many events were sent, how many were dropped since the last file, and the age of the oldest one.

The file you send (codelex-playtest-data.json) starts with: the file format and type, the app version and build number, the platform, your tester ID, the time of the export (phone clock), how many events were dropped (buffer full, older than 30 days, or failed a check) and how many are still waiting for the next file. Then come the events.

It never includes your name, email address, contacts, location, advertising ID, phone model or serial number, phone number, or any text you type.

To make this work, the app also keeps on your phone: your on/off choice and whether you saw the first-launch notice (kept after you switch off, so the app remembers “off”); a tiny “app is open” marker used to detect a crash; the time the “Send your playtest data?” reminder was last shown; for the match in progress, its random match id, its counts and the time it last went to the background; and which first-launch steps you reached. These are never put in a file you send. The marker, the reminder time, the match record and the steps are deleted when you switch recording off.

These events leave your phone only when you tap “Send playtest data” in Settings (or “Review and send” on the reminder), then “Share file…”. Your phone’s share menu opens, and you choose to email the file to titanfst00@gmail.com. Because the email shows your address, the file can then be linked to you. The app then asks “Did you send the file?”: if you tap “Yes, I sent it”, those events are cleared from your phone.

If you switch recording off (“Turn off and delete”), nothing more is recorded, and the unsent events, your tester ID, the marker, the reminder time, the match record and the steps are deleted from your phone. Note your tester ID first if you want us to delete data you already sent.

3.7 Survey reminder (when enabled; stays on your phone)

While “Record playtest stats” is on, the summary after your 1st and 3rd finished match may ask “Would you play CODELEX again?”, at most twice. Your answer, or the fact that you closed the question, is an event of section 3.6.

So that it never asks more than twice, the app keeps a small file on your phone with three counts, each 3 or less: matches finished, times the question was shown, and the match count when it was last shown. It holds no ID, no time and no answer. It is never put in any file you send and is excluded from backups. It stays on your phone when you switch recording off (so you are not asked again), and is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

3.8 Send match log (when enabled)

For bug reports, Settings offers “Send match log” (“For bug reports. Contains moves, no personal data.”), and so does the card shown when a saved match is damaged. It shares one file, codelex-match-log.json, holding the match in progress (or else the last finished one):

  • the file format and type, the app version and build number, and the platform;
  • the match settings, including the random seed, the rules and word-list versions;
  • all moves of both players (letters placed, tiles exchanged, passes) and the check values;
  • if the match no longer replays as it was saved, the first point where it differs;
  • or, if the saved match cannot be read at all, the saved file’s bytes as they are (the same kind of content).

It contains no tester ID, no match id, no time stamp, no file path and no device details. It works even when “Record playtest stats” is off.

You send it yourself by email to titanfst00@gmail.com. We then see your email address and anything your email app adds (such as your display name and the sending time).

3.9 Settings download (when enabled)

When your phone is online, the app will download a small settings file in the background, used to switch features on or off without a new version (for example, to pause a feature that has a bug). The request carries no identifier from the app. Like any internet request, it shows your IP address to the server that hosts the file. We will name that server in this policy before this feature is turned on.

3.10 Debrief calls (only if you opt in)

If you join a debrief call, the interviewer takes notes without your name. We do not record audio or video.

3.11 What we never collect

No account or password, no location, no contacts, no photos, no microphone, no advertising ID, no device model or serial numbers, no payment data, no text you type. The app shows no ads and contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software from other companies.

3.12 Owner-only test build

Malick BA also uses a separate test build, “CODELEX Diagnostics” (com.codelex.app.diagnostics), on his own phones to measure speed. It is never given to testers. Its report holds the phone model and Android version, a device class chosen by the owner, timings, and the app and code versions, with no tester ID.

6. Who can see it

At most three named analysts can see the received files. Only the playtest lead can see the sign-up details. The mailbox uses two-factor sign-in and no automatic forwarding.

Service providers:

  • Google, which provides the sign-up form (Google Forms), the playtest mailbox (Gmail) and the restricted storage (Google Drive), on Malick BA’s personal Google account.
  • Expo (EAS, by 650 Industries), which builds and hosts the app file and serves the private install link. It handles download data under its own privacy policy.
  • Your own email provider, when you choose to send a file (when enabled). You choose it; its terms apply.

There is no other third party.

7. Where it is stored

We do not store your data in the EU, or in any single country we chose. Data we receive (sign-up answers, emails and files) is kept in Malick BA’s personal (consumer) Google account: Gmail, Google Forms and Google Drive. Google may store and process it in several countries, including the United States, under its own terms and data-transfer commitments. So signing up or sending us a file may transfer your data outside your country. Expo, which serves the app download, is based in the United States.

8. Your rights

You can ask to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict its use, to object to uses based on our legitimate interest, and to receive it in a portable format. Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time; this does not affect what was done before.

How:

  • Data on your phone: switch off “Record playtest stats” (when enabled), or uninstall the app. We have no copy of it. The survey reminder counts and your settings go when you uninstall the app or clear its data.
  • Data you sent us: write to titanfst00@gmail.com. For playtest data, give us your tester ID (shown in Settings). For match logs, write from the address you sent them from. We delete the matching files.
  • Leaving the playtest: write to titanfst00@gmail.com; we remove you from the roster, except the record of your consent that we must keep.

We answer within one month. You can also complain to a data protection authority: the CNIL in France, the CDP (Commission de protection des données personnelles) in Senegal, or the authority where you live.

9. Security

Saved files on your phone sit in the app’s private storage. Files you send contain only what is listed above. On our side, access is limited to named people, and received files are checked as untrusted input before anyone opens them.
