CODELEX privacy policy (Phase 1 Android playtest)

Version playtest-privacy-v1.1 · 7 October 2026

In short

  • CODELEX is a word game about technology. This policy covers the Phase 1 playtest of the Android app (com.codelex.app) and the sign-up form for that playtest. It is not the policy of a public release.
  • The playtest is for adults (18+) who were invited through the sign-up form. The app is shared through a private install link only.
  • Today, nothing leaves your phone. The app has no account, no server, no ads, no analytics service and no crash reporting service. Your matches are saved on your phone so you can resume them.
  • When the next update ships (marked “when enabled” below), you will be able to send two files yourself, by email, with a tap: your playtest data (if the “Playtest data” setting is on) and a match log for bug reports. Nothing is ever sent automatically.

1. Who is responsible

Malick BA, an individual, is the controller of the personal data described here. Contact for any privacy question or request: titanfst00@gmail.com.

2. Who can take part

You must be 18 or older to take part. You confirm it when you sign up. The app is not meant for children. It is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you are under 18, please do not install the app. If we learn that someone under 18 took part, we remove them from the playtest and delete the data we hold about them. If you think this happened, write to titanfst00@gmail.com.

The app has no age check of its own, because it is only shared with the invited roster. A public version will have an age check before it is released.

3. What we collect, feature by feature

3.1 Signing up for the playtest (now)

Through the sign-up form (a Google Form), we collect: your name, your email address (which we use to send you the install link), a yes/no confirmation that you are 18 or older (not your birth date), your country, your preferred language (English or French), your phone type (Android standard or entry-level), whether you work in software, whether you want a debrief call, and the version and date of the consent you gave.

This list is kept apart from the game data. We never write the app’s random identifier into it.

3.2 Getting the app (now)

The app is not on Google Play. It is an Android app file (APK) built and hosted by Expo Application Services (EAS, run by 650 Industries, “Expo”). We send you a private install link. When you open it, Expo’s servers see the usual technical data of a download, such as your IP address and your device and browser information. Expo handles that data under its own privacy policy. We do not receive install or stability statistics from Google Play or from Expo.

3.3 Playing and resuming a match (now; stays on your phone)

To let you pick up a match where you left it, the app saves on your phone:

  • the match log: the moves played, the random seed of the match, the game and word-list versions, and check values used to detect a damaged file. It contains no time stamp, no identifier and nothing about you;
  • a snapshot of the match, to resume faster;
  • a small resume-safety counter, so that a damaged save shows a “start a new match” card instead of crashing.

Only the match in progress and the last finished match are kept. Older ones are deleted automatically.

3.4 “New to you” (now; stays on your phone)

After a match, the summary shows how many terms you played for the first time on this phone. For this, the app keeps on your phone the list of terms you have played (word ids only; no time, no identifier, nothing about the opponent).

3.5 Language (now; stays on your phone)

The app reads your phone’s language setting to show English or French. It does not store or send it.

3.6 Playtest data (when enabled)

A “Playtest data” setting will appear in Settings. It is on by default, because you agreed to it when you signed up; you can switch it off at any time. While it is on, the app records game events on your phone:

  • a random identifier created on your phone (shown in Settings; a new one is created if you reinstall the app);
  • for each event: an event id, the time on your phone’s clock, a session id, the app version, the platform (Android), the app language and a format version;
  • app opens (start-up time, type of network connection such as Wi-Fi, mobile or none, and whether the previous session ended in a crash) and time spent in the app;
  • matches started, finished, abandoned and resumed: opponent level, who played first, result, scores, how the match ended, number of turns, words, passes and exchanges, and match duration;
  • the terms played by you and by the bot (word ids, category, difficulty, points) and how long you thought;
  • rejected attempts: only the reason and the word length, never the letters you tried;
  • the bot’s thinking time and effort, in coarse ranges;
  • Knowledge Cards opened and how long you read them;
  • your answer to the “Would you play CODELEX again?” question;
  • how many events were sent or dropped.

It does not include your name, email, location, contacts, advertising ID, device model, phone number or any text you type.

These events leave your phone only when you tap “Send playtest data”. Your phone’s share menu opens, and you choose to email the file to titanfst00@gmail.com. Because the email shows your address, the file can then be linked to you.

If you switch the setting off, nothing more is recorded and the events still on your phone are deleted.

3.7 Send match log (when enabled)

For bug reports, Settings will offer “Send match log”. It shares the match in progress (or the last finished one) as a file with: the moves, the seed, the game and word-list versions, the check values, the app version and build number, and the platform. It contains no identifier, no time stamp and no device details, and it does not include the random identifier of section 3.6. It works even when “Playtest data” is off.

You send it yourself by email to titanfst00@gmail.com. The team then sees your email address and anything your email app adds (such as your display name and the sending time).

3.8 Settings download (when enabled)

When your phone is online, the app will download a small settings file in the background, used to switch features on or off without a new version (for example, to pause a feature that has a bug). The request carries no identifier from the app. Like any internet request, it shows your IP address to the server that hosts the file. We will name that server in this policy before this feature is turned on.

3.9 Debrief calls (only if you opt in)

If you join a debrief call, the interviewer takes notes without your name. We do not record audio or video.

3.10 What we never collect

No account or password, no location, no contacts, no photos, no microphone, no advertising ID, no device model or serial numbers, no payment data, no text you type. The app shows no ads and contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software from other companies.

4. Why we use it, and on what legal basis

Purposes and legal bases under the GDPR
What Why Legal basis (GDPR)
Sign-up details Check eligibility, invite you, contact you, keep a record of your consent Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)); keeping the record of consent: legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c), Art. 7(1))
Match log, snapshot, resume counter, “New to you” list on your phone Let you resume matches and show the summary. We never receive them Storing them on your phone is strictly necessary for the feature you use (ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3))
Playtest data (when enabled) Decide whether the game is fun and stable enough to keep building Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw in Settings
Match log email (when enabled) Reproduce and fix the bug you report Our legitimate interest in fixing bugs you report yourself (Art. 6(1)(f))
Settings download (when enabled) Switch features on or off safely Our legitimate interest in running the app safely (Art. 6(1)(f))
Debrief notes Understand what you liked and disliked Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a))

We do not use any of this for advertising, we never sell it, we do not use it to evaluate employees, and we make no automated decisions about you.

5. How long we keep it

Retention periods
Data How long
Match log and snapshot on your phone The match in progress and the last finished match only
“New to you” list on your phone Until you uninstall the app or clear its data
Playtest events on your phone (when enabled) Until you confirm they were sent, and at most 30 days (at most 5,000 events, oldest dropped first); deleted at once if you switch the setting off
Emails to our mailbox (when enabled) Attachments are moved to restricted storage (Google Drive) within 7 days, then the emails are deleted
Received playtest files and match logs Deleted 6 months after the playtest ends. Only anonymous totals are kept
Debrief notes Deleted 6 months after the playtest ends
Sign-up details 12 months after the playtest ends, as a record of your consent

Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on your phone. The app’s data is excluded from Android backups and from device-to-device transfer, so it does not end up in your Google backup.

6. Who can see it

At most three named analysts can see the received files. Only the playtest lead can see the sign-up details. The mailbox uses two-factor sign-in and no automatic forwarding.

Service providers:

  • Google, which provides the sign-up form (Google Forms), the playtest mailbox (Gmail) and the restricted storage (Google Drive).
  • Expo (EAS, by 650 Industries), which builds and hosts the app file and serves the private install link. It handles download data under its own privacy policy.
  • Your own email provider, when you choose to send a file (when enabled). You choose it; its terms apply.

There is no other third party.

7. Where it is stored

Data we receive is stored with Google, which may store and process it in several countries, including the United States, under its standard contractual clauses and data-transfer commitments. So signing up or sending us a file may transfer your data outside your country. Expo, which serves the app download, is also based in the United States.

8. Your rights

You can ask to access, correct or delete your data, to restrict its use, to object to uses based on our legitimate interest, and to receive it in a portable format. Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time; this does not affect what was done before.

How:

  • Data on your phone: switch off “Playtest data” (when enabled), or uninstall the app. We have no copy of it.
  • Data you sent us: write to titanfst00@gmail.com. For playtest data, give us the identifier shown in Settings. For match logs, write from the address you sent them from. We delete the matching files.
  • Leaving the playtest: write to titanfst00@gmail.com; we remove you from the roster, except the record of your consent that we must keep.

We answer within one month. You can also complain to a data protection authority: the CNIL in France, the CDP (Commission de protection des données personnelles) in Senegal, or the authority where you live.

9. Security

Saved files on your phone sit in the app’s private storage. Files you send contain only what is listed above. On our side, access is limited to named people, and received files are checked as untrusted input before anyone opens them.

10. Changes

We will tell testers by email before any change that affects what we collect, and we will update the version and date below. A public release of CODELEX will come with a new policy.
